# Arm 3 — What the network analysis found

**Date:** 2026-11-08 · **Status:** 19 of 52 queue rows DONE, 29 PENDING, 3 BLOCKED ·
**Findings:** 42 (31 live, 4 retracted, 7 corrected)

This is the integrated answer across every instrument that has reported, including the
group-specific networks run on a second machine (`grp-050`, `grp-051`, `eos-052`). It is
generated by hand, not by `build_index.R` — but like every other summary here, prefer the
per-finding files if they disagree.

---

## The question

> Do gene co-expression networks differ across healthy / bipolar-off-lithium /
> bipolar-on-lithium, and does any difference survive removing cell composition?

## The one-line answer

**The transcriptional signal in whole blood is lithium, not bipolar diagnosis — by a
factor of ~350 at the gene level. At the network level, every well-controlled test of
structural difference is either null or small, and cell composition accounts for most of
what remains.**

---

## 1. The gene-level asymmetry that frames everything

From `de_final/results/`, the same cohort the networks are built on:

| contrast | DEGs at BH < 0.05 |
|:--|--:|
| whole blood, **lithium** | **1,426** |
| whole blood, **bipolar diagnosis** | **4** |
| cell-type, lithium | 161 |
| cell-type, bipolar diagnosis | **0** |

This was not previously stated anywhere in the arm, and it reframes the whole project.
`grp-051` intended to test module overlap against *both* DEG lists; the bipolar one was
silently skipped by its own `if (length(degs) < 5) next` guard. The guard was right.

**Anything the arm reports about "disease" is working against a gene-level signal of four
genes.** Anything it reports about lithium has 1,426 behind it.

---

## 2. Module preservation: no difference (the best-controlled test)

`base-002-ceil` is the only experiment that scores **one** reference against **two**
groups under **one** instrument — the 67-module ctrl160 network, 500 permutations,
n = 74 both sides. Preconditions were enforced in code: identical module *sets*, matched
`nperms` and `n_test`.

| statistic | ceiling (held-out controls) | bp_nolith | paired test |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|
| **medianRank** | 34.0 | 33.0 | **p = 0.724** |
| rank-shift direction | — | 34 worse / 31 better / 2 tied | **sign test p = 0.804** |
| Zsummary | 25.07 | 31.05 | p = 4.8e-05, *impossible direction* |

The Zsummary result is discarded: it says patients exceed a split-half **ceiling**. Gold
moves opposite the modules (35.78 → 23.16 against 25.07 → 31.05), so it is Zsummary's
size dependence, not a scale shift.

**The negative is calibrated**, which is what makes it usable: per-module rank shift has
**sd 18.2**, with 21 of 67 modules moving ≥20 ranks *in both directions*. That is the
noise floor at n = 74. Any single-module claim in this arm must clear ±18 ranks.

---

## 3. Permutation null: a directional lithium effect, under-powered

`perm-031`, K = 20 draws of 74 random samples from all 474, against base-001's bp_lith:

- gold: observed 21.74 vs null mean 32.02 — **19 of 20 null draws exceed observed**
  (emp p = 0.05, BH 0.36, **not** significant)
- 44 of 72 modules (61%) less preserved in bp_lith than the null mean
- **4 modules fall below all 20 null draws**: purple (−15.84), blue (−10.04),
  pink (−6.44), grey60 (−5.64)

K = 20 caps the minimum empirical p at 0.05, so BH across 71 modules cannot clear FDR
except at p = 0. The direction is consistent; the power is not there. **This is the
strongest surviving hint of a real lithium-specific structural effect**, and it is a hint.

---

## 4. Hub genes: no difference, and hub identity is barely reproducible

From `grp-051`, top-200 hubs per network, 8 draws per contrast:

| contrast | median hubs shared of 200 | % |
|:--|--:|--:|
| **ceiling** control vs control | 58 | **29.0** |
| bipolar: control vs bp_nolith | 53.5 | 26.75 |
| lithium: bp_nolith vs bp_lith | 59 | 29.5 |

All three are the same. The ceiling is the finding: **even two halves of the same control
group share only 29% of their hub genes.** Hub identity on this data carries little
information, which corroborates an earlier observation that every module's top hub sits
at kME 0.95–0.98.

## 5. Partition agreement: disease contrasts differ, lithium does not

Also `grp-051`. This is the one instrument that separates anything:

| contrast | Jaccard | vs ceiling |
|:--|--:|:--|
| **ceiling** control vs control | 0.183 | — |
| control vs bp_nolith | 0.152 | **p = 0.010** |
| control vs bp_lith | 0.163 | **p = 0.0086** |
| bp_nolith vs bp_lith | 0.176 | p = 0.065 (ns) |

ARI tells the same story (ceiling 0.267; bipolar 0.181, p = 0.0009). I checked the obvious
confound — module counts differ by group (bp_nolith 83, control 64–69) and ARI is
cluster-count sensitive — but after adjusting for the count gap the contrast effect
(0.080) still dwarfs the count effect (0.0011 per module), and Jaccard is far less
count-sensitive and agrees.

**Caveats:** n = 8 comparisons per contrast and the draws share samples, so the nominal
p-values are optimistic. Effect sizes are small (0.183 → 0.152).

### This does not contradict §2

Preservation asks whether *control-defined modules stay coherent* in another group.
Partition agreement asks whether *independently derived partitions* match. A continuum
carved slightly differently by the clustering can move the second without moving the
first. Both can be true, and both are reported.

## 6. Composition: pervasive, but not the whole story

`grp-051b`, 69 modules, group-specific control network:

| test | modules at BH < 0.05 |
|:--|--:|
| composition-associated | **58 of 69** |
| lithium-associated (marginal) | 41 |
| diagnosis-associated | 29 |
| **lithium, after adjusting b1–b4** | **28** |

**This is in tension with finding 001**, which found 0 of 33 lithium associations
surviving composition adjustment on the fixed 234-control reference. The networks differ
(69 group-specific modules vs 33, and now 71 after the normalisation rebuild), so the two
are not directly comparable — but the tension is real and is **not resolved**. It is the
single most important open question in the arm.

## 7. Module enrichment

**Lithium DEG overlap** (`grp-051`): 15 of 69 modules at BH < 0.05, up to OR 11.4
(lightcyan OR 7.35, p 9e-45; orangered3 OR 11.41; steelblue OR 8.12).

**GO** (`grp-051`): turquoise (1,046 genes) is a transcription-factor module
(DNA-binding TF activity, FDR 1.3e-22); greenyellow is nucleotide binding.

**BPD, asked properly** (`analyses/019`, new today): a Fisher test on 4 DEGs is
meaningless, so I ran rank-based enrichment (Wilcoxon on −log10 p, no threshold):
**27 of 69 modules enriched for BPD at BH < 0.05**, against 21 of 69 for lithium.

So a *distributed, sub-threshold* BPD signal does exist — too weak per gene to survive
gene-level FDR, concentrated enough that module aggregation detects it.

**Load-bearing caveat:** module genes are co-expressed by construction, so their DE
statistics are correlated and a Wilcoxon that assumes independence is anti-conservative.
The honest null permutes *sample* labels and recomputes DE, which needs the `de_final`
pipeline. Until that is run, treat 27/69 as suggestive, not established.

---

## What would change the picture

1. **Resolve §6.** Re-run the module-trait composition adjustment on one fixed reference
   so finding 001's 0/33 and grp-051b's 28/69 are actually comparable.
2. **Raise perm-031 from K = 20 to K ≥ 200.** It is the only calibrated test of a
   lithium-specific effect and it is the one that is under-powered.
3. **A sample-label-permuted null for §7**, so the BPD enrichment has a legitimate null.

## What is settled

- The gene-level signal is lithium, not diagnosis (1,426 vs 4).
- Control-defined module preservation does not differ between controls and unmedicated
  patients, at a measured noise floor of ±18 ranks.
- Hub identity is too unstable on this data (29% ceiling) to support any claim.
- Cell composition is associated with 58 of 69 modules.
