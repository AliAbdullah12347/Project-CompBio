## The short answer

**About 40 % of lithium's effect on whole-blood gene expression runs through the cell
mixture.** The point estimate is a median proportion mediated of **0.396** on the
pipeline's 4-balance tree and **0.449** on the proposal's 5-balance tree, across the
genes with a detectable total effect. Correcting for the mediator being *estimated*
rather than measured raises it on **both** trees — to **0.587** (lineage) and **0.767**
(proposal) — and both remain lower bounds. The proposal tree's larger correction is a
symptom, not a strength: its balances are measured less reliably, so more is corrected
for. See section 5.

The proposal expected "a proportion mediated of around 0.5". **The answer brackets
that**: 0.396 uncorrected, 0.587 once the mediator's measurement error is accounted for.
Uncorrected, the direct effect is the larger single component; corrected, mediation is.

The 95 % bootstrap interval is **0.152 to 0.567** (lineage) and **0.236 to 0.630**
(proposal). So the mediated share is clearly **not zero** — 99.7 % of resamples put it
above zero — but it is **not established as "substantial"** either: the pre-declared
PM > 0.50 threshold is cleared in only 14.6 % of resamples (31.3 % on the proposal
tree), and 0.5 sits inside both intervals.

**Per-gene mediated effects are not individually significant, and this arm cannot make
them so.** That is a property of the design, established in advance, not a
disappointment about the data — see section 4.

**And one caveat that belongs in the abstract, not the appendix:** the proportion
mediated is *not itself evidence of mediation*. Shuffling the lithium labels produces
a median PM of 0.38 on the same genes — because PM is a ratio with its numerator
inside its denominator, it sits near 0.4 even under pure noise. The decomposition is
meaningful only because the total effect being decomposed is independently real.
Section 3a has the full argument.

## 1. Stage one — what lithium does to the cell mixture

The proposal predicted lithium raises **myeloid relative to lymphoid only**.

| tree | balance | contrast | effect | t | p | BH q |
|:--|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| lineage | b1 | gran+mono vs T+NK+B | +0.1558 | +2.00 | 0.0471 | 0.094 |
| lineage | b2 | gran vs mono | +0.1026 | +2.55 | 0.0116 | 0.046 |
| lineage | b3 | T vs NK+B | +0.0354 | +0.56 | 0.5780 | 0.588 |
| lineage | b4 | NK vs B | +0.0629 | +0.54 | 0.5876 | 0.588 |
| proposal | b1 | neut+mono vs cd4n+cd4m+NK+B | +0.1922 | +1.70 | 0.0914 | 0.229 |
| proposal | b2 | neut vs mono | +0.1020 | +2.38 | 0.0183 | 0.092 |
| proposal | b3 | cd4n+cd4m vs NK+B | +0.0245 | +0.15 | 0.8806 | 0.980 |
| proposal | b4 | cd4n vs cd4m | +0.0028 | +0.03 | 0.9799 | 0.980 |
| proposal | b5 | NK vs B | +0.0919 | +0.42 | 0.6732 | 0.980 |

**The prediction is supported, with a refinement.** Both myeloid-vs-lymphoid (b1,
+0.1558, p = 0.047) and granulocytes-vs-monocytes (b2, +0.1026, p = 0.012) move **upward**, as
the neutrophilia literature predicts (Kast 2008; Krebs et al. 2020). The two balances
**inside** the lymphoid compartment -- T vs NK+B and NK vs B -- do not move at all
(|t| = 0.56 and 0.54).

After BH across the four balances only **b2** survives (q = 0.046); b1 reaches q = 0.094.
The proposal's finer tree gives the same picture more weakly (b1 p = 0.091, b2 p = 0.018),
and adds that the CD4-naive-vs-memory split is completely flat (t = +0.03).

**On the word "only", which the feedback challenged:** it survives in the sense that
matters. Nothing shifts within the lymphoid compartment. What the data add is that the
myeloid shift is not uniform -- granulocytes rise *relative to monocytes* as well as
relative to lymphocytes. That is a sharper version of the hypothesis, not a
falsification of it, and it is exactly what a granulocyte-specific effect looks like
when expressed in balance coordinates.

## 2. The primary result

| | lineage (4 balances) | proposal (5 balances) |
|:--|--:|--:|
| genes tested | 12018 | 12018 |
| significant **total** effect (BH 5%) | 1272 | 1075 |
| significant **direct** effect (BH 5%) | 282 | 193 |
| significant **indirect** effect (BH 5%) | **0** | **3647** |
| genes with exposure-by-mediator interaction (BH 5%) | 1 | 0 |
| median proportion mediated | 0.396 | 0.449 |
| share with PM > 0.50 | 30.3% | 38.7% |

The two trees agree closely on **estimates** — per-gene NIE correlate at 0.980 — and
disagree completely on **counts** (0 vs 3647 significant). Section 3 explains why, and
why the counts should not be reported as a finding.

### Four-way decomposition (VanderWeele 2014), lineage tree

| component | meaning | mean &#124;size&#124; | share |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|
| CDE | neither mediation nor interaction | 0.12225 | 53.6% |
| INTref | interaction only | 0.00867 | 3.8% |
| INTmed | mediation **and** interaction | 0.01288 | 5.7% |
| PIE | pure mediation | 0.08410 | 36.9% |

Mediation (PIE + INT_med) accounts for **42.6 %** of the decomposed effect.

### External validation against the differential-expression arm

The mediation **total** effect is, by construction, the quantity the DE arm already
estimated. The two were computed by different code, different models (21 parameters here
against ~12 there) and different gene sets, months apart. They agree:

| | |
|:--|--:|
| correlation of DE log2FC with mediation TE | **0.9985** |
| sign agreement | 98.0 % |
| DE lithium genes inside our primary set | 1,376 |
| recovered by the mediation total effect | 1,224 (**89.0 %**), Jaccard 0.860 |

The small shortfall is expected, not a discrepancy: the mediation outcome model spends
nine extra degrees of freedom on the mediators and their interactions, so it has slightly
less power on the same data. **The decomposition is splitting a total effect that was
independently established, not inventing one.**

### Was the interaction worth allowing?

The feedback required testing it before reporting any proportion mediated. **Only 1
gene of 12018 shows a lithium-by-cell-mix interaction at BH 5 %** — so the proposal's
original product-of-coefficients assumption was, for practical purposes, defensible.
It was still right to test rather than assume: the two estimators differ by 8.8 % of
the median effect and **disagree in sign for 2.3 % of genes**.

## 3. The one result that must not be over-read

The number of genes with a "significant" mediated effect is **0 on one tree and 3647 on
the other**, from estimates correlating at 0.980. Both numbers are arithmetically
correct. Benjamini-Hochberg is a step-up procedure, so when the signal is diffuse and
weak a small shift in the whole p-value distribution flips it between almost nothing and
thousands. The smallest p-value is 4.1e-03 (lineage) and 9.5e-04 (proposal); neither is
anywhere near the 4.2e-06 a single gene would need.

**The permutation null settles it.** With lithium labels shuffled 1,000 times:

| statistic | observed | null median | null 95th | null max | permutation p |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| lineage — genes with NIE at BH 5% | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 7150.000 | 1.0000 |
| lineage — genes with TE at BH 5% | 1272.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 1266.000 | 0.0010 |
| lineage — max &#124;z&#124; for NIE | 2.874 | 1.749 | 2.801 | 4.170 | 0.0410 |
| lineage — median proportion mediated | 0.396 | 0.173 | 0.585 | 0.794 | 0.1818 |
| proposal — genes with NIE at BH 5% | 3647.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 6717.000 | 0.0060 |
| proposal — genes with TE at BH 5% | 1075.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 993.000 | 0.0010 |
| proposal — max &#124;z&#124; for NIE | 3.305 | 1.945 | 2.919 | 3.970 | 0.0130 |
| proposal — median proportion mediated | 0.449 | 0.141 | 0.531 | 0.706 | 0.1724 |

Three things to read off that table.

**The total effect is unambiguous.** 1,272 genes observed against a null that reaches
1266 at its most extreme and has a median of 0 (permutation p = 0.001). Lithium
undeniably changes whole-blood expression.

**The mediated-gene COUNT is uninterpretable, and the null proves it.** Shuffling the
labels — where lithium does nothing by construction — still produced as many as
**7150 "significant" mediated genes** in a single replicate (6717 on the proposal tree),
while the median null is 0. A statistic whose null ranges from 0 to seven thousand
cannot support a claim of the form "we found N mediated genes". This is the same
step-up instability that made the two trees disagree, now measured directly.

**There is nonetheless real mediated signal.** The strongest per-gene evidence,
max |z|, sits beyond the null: 2.87 against a 95th percentile of 2.80 (permutation
p = 0.041; 0.013 on the proposal tree). Weak, but not nothing.

> **One permutation comparison in that table should be ignored.** The "median
> proportion mediated" row recomputed the statistic inside each replicate over that
> replicate's own significant genes — and under the null almost nothing is
> significant, so it was defined in only 32 of 1,000 replicates. A null built from
> 3 % of replicates, selected because they happened to produce significant genes, is
> not evidence. `04b_perm_pm.R` redoes it properly on a fixed gene set; see below.

### 3a. Permutation null for the headline quantity, done properly


Gene set held fixed at
the genes significant in the real analysis, so the statistic is defined in every
replicate:

| tree | observed median PM | null median | null 95 % range | permutation p |
|:--|--:|--:|:--:|--:|
| lineage | 0.396 | 0.383 | -0.083 to 0.897 | **0.492** |
| proposal | 0.449 | 0.380 | -0.238 to 1.051 | **0.409** |

**Read this carefully, because it is the most important caveat in the arm.**

The observed proportion mediated, 0.396, is *indistinguishable* from what the same
genes produce when lithium is shuffled and therefore does nothing at all (null
median 0.383, permutation p = 0.49). That is not a bug and not a null result —
it is a property of the statistic. PM = NIE / (NDE + NIE) is a ratio whose numerator
appears in its own denominator, so even with pure noise in both paths it lands near
0.4, not near 0. **The proportion mediated has essentially no power to demonstrate
that mediation exists.**

What follows from that, precisely:

- **PM cannot be used as evidence that lithium acts through the cell mixture.** Any
  write-up that says "40 % mediated, therefore composition matters" is unsupported.
- **PM is still a meaningful decomposition**, because the total effect being
  decomposed is independently real (1,272 genes, permutation p = 0.001, and it
  reproduces the DE arm at r = 0.9985). Given a genuine total effect, splitting it
  40 / 60 is a legitimate description — the bootstrap pins it to 0.396 ± 0.106,
  far tighter than the null's -0.08-to-0.90 spread.
- **The evidence that mediation is non-zero comes from elsewhere**, and it is weak:
  lithium moves the cell mixture (granulocytes vs monocytes, p = 0.012), and the
  strongest per-gene mediated effect exceeds its permutation null (p = 0.041).

The defensible claim is therefore: *lithium's transcriptomic effect is real and large;
lithium does shift the cell mixture; a substantial share of the effect is consistent
with running through that shift; and this design cannot establish the share more
sharply than "somewhere between a seventh and three fifths".*

**Report the proportion mediated, not the gene count.** The proportion is stable across
trees (0.396 vs 0.449), across deconvolutions (0.396 vs 0.389), across zero-replacement constants
and across weighting. The count is not stable across anything.

## 3b. Bootstrap — the interval on the headline number

B = 1000, resampling the 226 **subjects** with replacement, refitting TMM, voom, the
mediator models and the outcome models inside every replicate. The estimand is the
pre-declared one: median proportion mediated over the genes with a significant total
effect, with that gene set fixed from the primary analysis so the interval carries
uncertainty in the effects rather than in a moving selection rule.

| tree | genes | median PM | 95 % percentile CI | bootstrap SE | replicates > 0.5 |
|:--|--:|--:|:--:|--:|--:|
| lineage | 1272 | 0.396 | 0.152 to 0.567 | 0.1057 | 14.6 % |
| proposal | 1075 | 0.449 | 0.236 to 0.630 | 0.0976 | 31.3 % |

**The delta method is anticonservative here**, which is why this matters: its standard
errors run at 92 % of the bootstrap's (lineage).
The validation suite had already flagged this on simulated data (95 % intervals
covering 93.0 % and 94.4 %); the real data confirm it. **Quote the bootstrap interval,
not the delta-method one.**

Per gene, 4917 of 12018 (40.9 %) have a bootstrap percentile interval for the indirect
effect that excludes zero **before** any multiplicity correction, and 0 after BH.

## 4. Power — why no amount of sequencing would fix this

The feedback asked for a power calculation at n = 226. It is the most consequential
number in the arm.

`Var(NIE)` splits into an outcome-model part and a mediator-model part. **79.5 % comes
from the mediator models** — the lithium-to-cell-mix link. That part does not shrink
with deeper sequencing or more genes. It shrinks only with **more subjects**.

| | |
|:--|--:|
| median observed &#124;NIE&#124; | 0.0393 |
| minimum detectable NIE, 80% power, uncorrected 5% | 0.0645 |
| minimum detectable NIE, 80% power, Bonferroni | 0.1254 |
| **largest attainable &#124;z&#124; if expression were measured perfectly** | **2.92** |
| &#124;z&#124; needed for Bonferroni across 12018 genes | 4.60 |
| subjects that would be needed | **~563** instead of 226 |

You cannot be more certain about a mediated effect than about the exposure-to-mediator
link that carries it, and with t ≈ 2 on the best balance that ceiling binds hard. **This
was knowable before any data were analysed and is reported whichever way the estimates
came out.**

### S1 in full, because it is the one everybody will ask about

The cell fractions are estimated **from the expression matrix**, so for the 547 LM22
signature genes the mediator is nearly a function of the outcome. Those genes are held
out of the primary set (350 survive filtering) and analysed separately.

| | non-marker genes (12,018) | LM22 markers (350) |
|:--|--:|--:|
| median proportion mediated | 0.396 | 0.355 |
| median NIE | -0.01526 | -0.03404 |

Markers have **larger** absolute mediated effects, as circularity predicts — but their
total effects are larger too, so the *proportion* mediated is slightly **lower**
(0.355 vs 0.396). Circularity is visible in the raw effect sizes and does **not** inflate the
headline quantity. Excluding them remains the right call, but the exclusion turns out
not to have been load-bearing.

### S3 in full — the correction is directionally certain and numerically not

The mediator is *estimated*, so classical measurement error attenuates its
coefficient and understates mediation. The proposal commits to correcting this by
regression calibration (Valeri, Lin & VanderWeele 2014) and reporting the uncorrected
figure as a lower bound.

Two things had to be fixed to do this honestly:

1. **The full multivariate correction is inadmissible.** The reliabilities imply more
   measurement error than the observed balance covariance can support: the implied
   true-score covariance has two negative eigenvalues. Only 56 % of the implied
   correction can be applied before it stops being a covariance matrix at all.
2. **At that boundary it is numerically unstable.** The attenuation matrix has an
   eigenvalue near 0.01, so inverting it amplifies by ~100x in one direction:

| correction strength c | condition number | median PM |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 0.000 | 1.0 | 0.396 |
| 0.100 | 1.2 | 0.414 |
| 0.200 | 1.5 | 0.438 |
| 0.300 | 2.0 | 0.464 |
| 0.400 | 3.2 | 0.497 |
| 0.500 | 7.9 | 0.566 |
| 0.561 | 91.9 | 0.877 |

**Both trees, since the correction depends on how well each mediator is measured:**

| tree | balances | median reliability (ICC) | PM uncorrected | PM corrected |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| lineage (4 balances) | 4 | 0.614 | 0.396 | **0.587** |
| proposal (5 balances) | 5 | 0.474 | 0.449 | **0.767** |

The correction raises the mediated proportion on **both** trees, which is the point
that matters. But the proposal tree moves further (+0.32 against +0.19) **because its
mediator is measured worse**, not because it finds more mediation: its median
reliability is 0.474 against 0.614, and its CD4-naive-vs-memory balance has an ICC of
just **0.200** — two deconvolutions of the same blood barely agree on it. A correction
driven by a near-unmeasurable coordinate deserves less trust, not more, so the lineage
figure of 0.587 is the one to quote.

A single number taken from the last row would be an artefact of where the
admissibility boundary happens to fall. **We therefore report the stable per-balance
correction (t2_k / lambda_k), which gives 0.587**, and show the sweep rather than hiding it.
The *size* of the correction is uncertain; its *direction* is not — every version moves
the proportion mediated up. And because both deconvolutions behind the reliabilities
share the LM22 signature, the reliabilities are themselves overstated, so even the
corrected figure is conservative.

The total effect is held **exactly** fixed throughout (max |TE_corrected − TE| = 0),
because TE is the coefficient of a model containing no mediator and so cannot be
changed by measurement error in one. The correction only moves effect between the
direct and indirect paths.

### E-value (S4)

| quantity | median E-value | at the top gene |
|:--|--:|--:|
| total effect | 1.305 | 2.407 |
| indirect (mediated) effect | 1.233 | 1.531 |
| total effect, CI limit | 1.000 | 2.146 |

An unmeasured confounder would need associations of about **1.30** with both lithium use
and gene expression, beyond the measured covariates, to explain away the typical total
effect. **For scale, age — which we do adjust for — sits at 2.33 on the same scale.** So
a confounder no stronger than age would suffice. Illness severity, mood state and other
psychotropics are all plausible at that magnitude and **none is recorded in this dataset**.
This is the single largest threat to the arm and it cannot be resolved with these data.

## 6. Exploratory analyses (labelled exploratory, never confirmatory)

**T1 — age as exposure, per 10 years.** Age barely moves the cell mixture (all four
balances |t| < 1.4), so there is little for it to mediate: 90 of 12018 genes show a
significant total effect of age and the median proportion mediated among them is only
**0.084**, against 0.396 for lithium. Lithium's effect on expression is compositional in a way
age's is not.

**T2 — does age's mediated effect differ between lithium users and non-users?** The
proposal, after Salarda et al. (2021), expected it to be *larger* in users. It is not:
the users' mediated effect is larger in absolute size for only **24.6 %** of genes, and
**0 genes** show a significant difference.

**This null is uninformative and must be reported as such.** The smallest detectable
difference at 80 % power is 0.09978 against an observed median of 0.01726 — the design could
only detect a difference **5.8x larger** than the one present. With 74 non-users this
comparison was underpowered before it was run, which is exactly what the feedback
anticipated.

## 7. What to carry into the paper

1. **Headline:** roughly 0.396 of lithium's whole-blood transcriptomic effect is mediated by
   cell composition, rising to **0.587** once the mediator's measurement error is
   accounted for (the proposal tree gives 0.449 rising to 0.767). Both are lower
   bounds, and they bracket the 0.5 the proposal expected.
2. **The direct effect is not negligible** — it is the single largest component (53.6 % of
   the four-way decomposition). Lithium does more than rearrange the cell mixture.
3. **Do not report a count of mediated genes.** Report the proportion and its interval.
4. **State the power ceiling**: ~563 subjects would be needed for per-gene significance.
5. **Correct two statements in the proposal** — see `PRESPEC.md` §6: the mediator tree
   actually used, and the claim that ILR improved cross-method consistency (it did not;
   aggregation from 22 types to 5 lineages did).
6. **Drop the word "only"** from the myeloid-vs-lymphoid hypothesis: the balance that
   moves most clearly is granulocytes vs monocytes.

## 8. Limitations

- No lithium dose, no other medications, no illness severity, no BMI — see the E-value.
- The mediator is **estimated from the same matrix** as the outcome (S1 bounds this).
- Natural effects need no mediator-outcome confounder affected by the exposure; this is
  **not verifiable here**.
- Observational data: everything is associational under a causal interpretation.
- The ICCs behind the calibration overstate reliability (shared LM22 signature), so the
  corrected estimate is itself conservative.
- The delta method is ~10 % anticonservative here; bootstrap intervals are preferred.
