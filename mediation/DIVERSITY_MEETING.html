Alpha and beta diversity — meeting notes

Main results

  1. Lithium makes blood gene expression less diverse — through the cell mix. Confirmed.
  2. The cause is more granulocytes, not cells behaving differently.
  3. Lithium shifts people's whole expression profile; most of that shift goes with the cell mix. Suggestive.
  4. Bipolar off lithium looks like controls.

Key details

Alpha, via cell mix ≈ 3.4% fewer "effective genes"; p = 0.020, corrected 0.041
Where it comes from 95% is the blood reweighting toward granulocytes
Transcriptome size by cell type granulocytes ~1,400 effective genes · monocytes ~2,100 · T and B ~3,600 · NK ~4,800
Cell mix evenness less even on lithium, p = 0.003
Beta, shift p = 0.0003; people not more or less varied (p = 0.50)
Beta, mediation 67% shared with cell mix; formal test p = 0.080 (main tree), 0.041 (proposal tree)
Pathways no direct effect left once cell mix is accounted for (p = 0.35)
Bipolar no shift, no diversity change; patients possibly more varied among themselves (p = 0.011, 0.013) — exploratory

How the results connect

  1. Lithium raises granulocytes relative to monocytes → the cell mix gets less even (p = 0.003).
  2. Granulocytes have by far the narrowest transcriptome (~1,400 effective genes, a third of a lymphocyte's).
  3. So blood with more granulocytes has a less diverse average transcriptome — even if no cell changes. It is arithmetic of mixing.
  4. That is exactly what we measured: the indirect effect is significant, 95% of the drop is reweighting, and nothing is detectable inside cell types.
  5. The same shift is the main way lithium moves whole profiles: the top axes lithium moves track granulocyte fraction and are ~70–80% mediated (beta).

What it means

  • It backs up the per-gene result. Gene by gene we estimated ~40% mediated but could not prove it. Asked once for the whole transcriptome, the cell-mix route is confirmed. Same answer, now with evidence.
  • Why the indirect effect is significant when the total is not: lithium moves essentially one thing in the cell mix (granulocytes vs monocytes). The indirect effect zooms in on it; tests of the whole mix or the whole effect get diluted. Same reason the beta mediation test is borderline.
  • Composition dominates, but direct effects exist. Globally and per pathway, lithium's effect is compositional; gene by gene, 245 genes keep a direct effect (e.g. TSPAN2). Reading: lithium's cell-intrinsic effects hit specific genes, not whole biological programmes.
  • For the project's question: a blood "signature" in lithium-treated patients largely reflects a shifted cell mix — which fits the premise of Arm 1 (cell-mix ratios predicting lithium nearly as well as all genes), to be tested there.
  • Bipolar: no trace in diversity or cell mix, matching the DE arm. The "patients more varied" pattern is a lead to mention, not a finding.

Caveats: effects are small; the data are observational (a confounder as strong as age could matter); the cell-type split rests on estimated profiles; the bipolar result is exploratory.
